## The part everyone skips: who is actually paying you

Cash back isn't a discount. The retailer never lowers its price for you. Retailers already spend money on affiliate marketing — paying publishers a commission on referred sales — and ShopBack is one of those publishers. Click through ShopBack to a merchant, and a tracking cookie tags your session. If you buy, the merchant's affiliate network confirms the sale and pays ShopBack a commission, usually a percentage of your order value. ShopBack keeps a slice to run the business and passes the rest to you.

> Your checkout price is unchanged. The retailer is paying out of a marketing budget it was spending anyway. ShopBack's own FAQ is blunt about the consequence: no subscription, no membership tiers, no minimum spend, and no withdrawal fee on standard payouts.

Because the money comes from a marketing budget rather than from you, three things can earn on a single order without cancelling each other out: a merchant promo code, your credit card's own rewards, and the cashback. They're funded by three different parties.

## What the new-member bonus actually requires

The signup bonus is the reason most people search for a ShopBack link in the first place, so it's worth being precise about it. The commonly promoted figure is **$15 for the invited friend**, and independent deal blogs reported it at that level repeatedly through late 2025 and into 2026. The referring account earns a separate, usually larger bonus — one review site logged $35 per qualified referral at its last update. Both numbers move, so treat them as directional and read the live invite page before you count on a specific amount.

The conditions are where people lose the money, and they're published rather than hidden. Per ShopBack's own refer-a-friend terms:

- You have to sign up through the unique referral link or apply the referral code at registration. Signing up normally and mentioning it later doesn't work.
- The referred account needs a valid purchase of **$5 or more**, either as one order or combined across several, and must update its cashback withdrawal details — PayPal or bank — **within 180 days** of signing up.
- The referred friend's first purchase must not be stacked with other new-customer promotions.
- Each referred person must sign up on a unique device. Self-referrals and referral links posted on a merchant's own Facebook or forum page are disqualifying, and repeat violations can close an account.

There's also a sequencing detail that catches people out. Your $15 bonus does not sit there withdrawable on its own. ShopBack's terms state you can withdraw the referral bonus once you've accumulated at least **$5 in confirmed cashback that isn't referral bonus** — while the general withdrawal threshold is **$10 in confirmed cashback**. In other words, the bonus arrives, but it only becomes cash once you've actually shopped through the platform and that cashback has cleared.

## Pending, then confirmed: the 30-to-90-day stretch

Cashback shows up in your account as *Pending* within roughly 48 hours of a qualifying purchase. It becomes *Confirmed* — and therefore withdrawable — only after the merchant's return window closes. For retail that's typically 30 to 90 days. For travel, it confirms after the trip is completed, not after you book.

That gap is the single biggest source of "ShopBack is a scam" reviews, and it's structural rather than suspicious. Every cashback portal works this way, because the merchant has the right to reverse a commission on a return, cancellation, or suspected fraud. A $400 laptop bought today is a pending line item for most of a quarter.

Where this actually matters: if you're judging the platform in month one based on what you can withdraw, you're reading a number that hasn't finished moving yet.

Rates can also differ between the app and the website for the same store, which is why the terms live on the merchant page rather than in a general help article. ## The tracking checklist that prevents most missing-cashback tickets

Most failed cashback isn't a platform problem. It's a cookie problem. Run this before you buy:

1. **Log in first.** A cashback click-through from a logged-out session has nothing to credit.
2. **Turn off ad blockers, content blockers, and aggressive privacy extensions** for the ShopBack and merchant domains, or allowlist them.
3. **Don't run other coupon sites or price-comparison tabs alongside the purchase.** Opening a competitor's coupon page mid-session can overwrite the tracking cookie.
4. **Start and finish on the same device and the same browsing session.** App to desktop mid-checkout breaks attribution.
5. **One click-through, one order.** If you want to place a second order, return to ShopBack and click through again.
6. **Only use promo codes the merchant's ShopBack page lists.** An unlisted code found elsewhere is a standard reason for rejection in merchant terms.
7. **Don't edit the merchant URL manually** between the click-through and checkout.
8. **Keep the order confirmation.** If cashback doesn't appear, you'll file a missing-cashback claim with the merchant name, order number and date, order subtotal, device, browser version, and whether you applied a code — the exact fields ShopBack's US support asks for. Fewer back-and-forths that way.

For larger purchases — appliances, electronics, travel — it's worth the extra 30 seconds of being disciplined about all of the above. Those are the transactions where the difference is real money rather than pocket change.
